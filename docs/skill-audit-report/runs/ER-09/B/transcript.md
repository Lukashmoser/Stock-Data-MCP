## Long ideas

**1. NVIDIA (NVDA): long, the best growth-adjusted FCF in the group**
- Rule of 40 is 125.3, the highest in the group by about 32 points. EV/FCF of 43.3x is *below* META (47.2x), MSFT (59.7x) and GOOGL (78.5x), which all grow far more slowly. That is roughly 0.35x EV/FCF per point of Rule-of-40, the cheapest ratio in the universe.
- Revenue went from 44,062 to 96,221 per quarter in five quarters. The 41.9% FCF margin comes with net cash of about 23,220 (excluding 42,783 of strategic equity stakes).
- **Key risk:** cash conversion is slipping. In FQ2 FY27, operating cash flow fell to 24,077 from 50,344 as receivables rose 40,710 → 63,059 (+55% q/q against +18% revenue growth). If customers' capex budgets plateau, or receivables keep outrunning revenue, both the growth and the FCF parts of the thesis break together.

**2. Adobe (ADBE): long, deep value inside a quality franchise**
- At 8.8x EV/FCF it is the cheapest name in the screen, with a 40.8% FCF margin and Rule of 40 of 52.8 (4th). The price of 236.28 is 35% below its 52-week high of 363.70.
- Revenue growth is steady at 12.0% and quarterly revenue rose every quarter in the window. Diluted share count fell about 10% year over year (439 → 396 million) on buybacks of about 8,900 TTM, an FCF yield of about 11.5% being returned to holders.
- **Key risk:** generative-AI tools erode Creative Cloud seat pricing and new-user acquisition. The market is pricing that as a terminal decline. If net-new ARR decelerates sharply, the low multiple is a value trap.

**3. Salesforce (CRM): long, re-rating candidate with a self-funded buyback**
- At 14.8x EV/FCF (2nd cheapest) with a 34.5% FCF margin and Rule of 40 of 45.7, it scores above AAPL, MSFT and META on Rule of 40 at a quarter to a third of their multiples.
- Management bought back about 27,248 of stock in FQ1 FY27. Basic share count fell from about 960 to 820 million year over year, which mechanically lifts FCF per share by about 17%.
- **Key risk:** leverage and quality of growth. Long-term debt rose from 10,439 to 39,288 to fund the buyback. Part of the 11.2% growth is acquired (about 8,236 of acquisitions in FQ4 FY26, likely Informatica), so organic growth is probably high single digits. Agentic AI could also compress per-seat CRM pricing.

*Honourable mention:* AVGO (Rule of 40 92.9 at 44.2x) is about as cheap as NVDA on growth-adjusted FCF, but has about 35,444 of net debt and more customer concentration.

## Short idea

**Intel (INTC): short, a turnaround priced as if it were already done**
- At 198.2x EV/FCF it is the most expensive name in the screen. Rule of 40 is 12.4 (last), the FCF margin is 5.0%, and TTM growth is 7.5%. It has net debt of about 20,810, and a GAAP net loss of about 11,033 in the June quarter.
- The stock is up from a 52-week low of 32.89 to 102.81 (about 3.1x). Market cap of about 540,218 is 9.5x TTM revenue, a premium to QCOM (about 4.3x) and IBM (about 3.1x), which have much better cash generation. Shares outstanding rose from about 4,369 to 5,043 million in a year (+15% dilution from strategic and government equity raises).
- **Key risk:** a large external 14A/18A foundry customer win, or more strategic or government capital, could rewrite the narrative regardless of current FCF. Short-squeeze and policy-support risk is high, so size it small and/or express it as a pair (short INTC / long QCOM or AMD).

**Next steps:** ADBE and CRM first (cheapest quality; check net-new ARR, cRPO and organic growth). Then NVDA receivables and DSO diligence ahead of the FQ3 print. For INTC, check short interest and borrow cost before sizing.

### 3. Estimates and gaps
- **Defaults chosen:**
  - TTM = last 4 reported fiscal quarters.
  - Growth = TTM vs prior TTM (fiscal-quarter aligned).
  - FCF = operating cash flow − capex. Finance-lease principal is not deducted, and SBC is not deducted.
  - EV = market cap (Yahoo) + total debt (current + non-current notes) − cash − current marketable debt securities. Operating and finance lease liabilities are excluded. Long-term marketable securities (e.g. AAPL's) are excluded. NVDA's 42,783 of marketable equity securities are excluded from cash.
  - Negative-FCF names are ranked last on EV/FCF.
  - Composite is an equal-weight average of 4 ranks.
  - Long and short picks are judgement calls informed by the composite (quality at a reasonable price), not a strict top-3 / bottom-1. AVGO was ranked 2nd but not picked, and ORCL and AMZN were ranked below INTC on the composite but not chosen as the short.
