References: R0 and R0c have no monthly MSFT prices or month-end DGS10 history (R0c gives only DGS10 of 5.19% on 2026-10-14), so the market inputs can't be checked against the reference. I checked the structural rows directly in the files with openpyxl, and compared the two arms' Inputs tabs value by value. Runs: arm A 2026-10-16 03:35–03:38 UTC, arm B 03:35–03:36 UTC (2026-10-15 PT).

Cross-check: both Inputs tabs have 61 month-end rows, 2021-09-30 to 2026-09-30, and **all 61 MSFT adjusted closes and 61 yields agree between the arms** (within 0.01 / 0.005). B used stock-data get_price_history (Yahoo) and get_macro_series (FRED DGS10, end of period). Because both inputs agree, the identical correlation outputs (−0.257 and +0.330) are well supported, even without an R0 value.

## 4. Most important difference

The deliverables are equivalent: the same 61×2 inputs, the same live-formula Calc sheet, and the same correlations (−0.257 full period, +0.330 latest 12 months). The difference is effort and fragility. Arm A needed 18 WebFetch calls and two re-fetches to get month-end values right, because the first reads of the 2024 and 2025 Treasury files picked the first day of each month. Five FRED attempts were unusable. Every input was extracted by a summarizing model, so A marked the prices Medium confidence. Arm B got both series in two tool calls in about a minute. Its yield series is FRED DGS10, the standard source, and the Treasury par yields A used match it. Accuracy can't separate the arms here; the speed and reliability of the data path can.
