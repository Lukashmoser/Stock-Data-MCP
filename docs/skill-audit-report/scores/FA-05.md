## 5. Most important difference

Both arms found all seven planted errors, and the data errors were not the gap the protocol expected. Arm A caught D1 and D3 by checking against Microsoft's FY2026 earnings release, which carries FY2025 comparatives and FY2026 cash. The difference is how the inputs were verified. Arm B checked all 136 historical input cells against SEC XBRL from get_financials (134 matched). Arm A checked FY2023-FY2024 and most FY2025 lines against memory, and those happened to be right. B also flagged that the model's price was a day stale, which A could not do. On side calculations, A was the more careful arm: it recalculated a fixed copy and got both the corrected E10 plug (48,594) and FY2031 cash (351,702) right. B worked these by hand and got both wrong, though neither appears in its key-figure table.
